These are the 5 most relevant figures from the toy work so far.

1. `fig1_toy_baseline_nashmtl.png`
This is the plain Nash-MTL reference. It matters because every other toy result should be understood relative to this baseline.

2. `fig2_toy_replicator_trajectory.png`
This is the tuned replicator version of the same toy setup. It matters because it shows that the new method is not just running; it actually changes the optimization path.

3. `fig3_toy_weight_evolution.png`
This is the best “mechanism” figure. It shows the three levels of the method:
- replicator shares
- Nash weights
- final weights
This is the cleanest proof that the outer scheduler is active and stable on the representative case.

4. `fig4_toy_old_collapse.png`
This is the old failure mode. It is important because it gives you a concrete before-state instead of only claiming that the method was unstable.

5. `fig5_toy_new_stable.png`
This is the repaired after-state on the same hard case. It matters because it shows the old collapse mode has actually been replaced by stable behavior, not just hidden.

Taken together, these figures support three claims:

- plain Nash-MTL is the stable but non-adaptive reference behavior
- replicator-Nash-MTL now changes weighting behavior in a controlled and interpretable way
- the old collapse problem on the hard toy case was real, and the repaired version avoids it

What these figures do NOT prove:

- they do not prove benchmark superiority on QM9 or NYUv2
- they do not prove that replicator-Nash-MTL is empirically better on real data

What they ARE good for:

- method/mechanism validation
- stability story
- toy ablation and debugging evidence
