Method

- Proposed method: replicator-augmented Nash-MTL (`replicator_nashmtl`).
- Core idea:
  - retain Nash-MTL as the inner per-step task-weighting mechanism
  - add a slower outer replicator scheduler that adjusts task influence over time
  - use the replicator to modulate Nash rather than replace it
- Inner weighting mechanism:
  - Nash weights are computed at every optimization step
  - these weights represent the bargaining-based multi-task compromise at the current step
- Outer scheduler mechanism:
  - replicator shares are updated every `k` steps instead of every step
  - the scheduler uses EMA-smoothed task signals rather than raw instantaneous losses
  - the preferred signal is `qm9_proxy`, which blends:
    - current relative task loss
    - smoothed under-improvement
    - relative task underperformance
- Final weighting rule:
  - `nash_weights` provide the base task weighting
  - `replicator_shares` provide a slower adaptive reallocation signal
  - `final_weights` are produced by modulating the Nash solution using the replicator state
- Intended effect:
  - preserve Nash-MTL fairness/conflict handling at the step level
  - add slower adaptive emphasis for tasks that fall behind over training
- Final tuned toy settings:
  - `--scheduler-signal qm9_proxy`
  - `--debug-init-preset representative`
  - `--replicator-update-every 1`
  - `--replicator-lr 0.05`
  - `--replicator-uniform-mix 0.05`
  - `--replicator-modulation-strength 0.3`
  - `--toy-payoff-temperature 0.1`
  - `--toy-payoff-gain 5.0`
  - `--n-epochs 220`

Experimental Design

- Primary validation strategy:
  - first validate mechanism and stability on the toy setup
  - then attempt a small real-data confidence pass on QM9
- Toy experiment goals:
  - verify that the outer scheduler is active
  - confirm that it changes task weighting relative to plain Nash-MTL
  - confirm that the repaired design avoids the old collapse mode
- Toy baseline:
  - plain `nashmtl`
- Toy proposed method:
  - `replicator_nashmtl`
- Representative toy cases:
  - `init=0`: strongest nontrivial mechanism case
  - `init=1`: hard stability case, previously collapse-prone
- Stability checks recorded automatically:
  - `shares_positive`
  - `shares_normalized`
  - `shares_moved`
  - `no_one_hot_collapse`
  - `final_weights_differ_from_nash`
  - `max_share_deviation`
  - `max_final_vs_nash_diff`
  - `max_relative_loss_gap`
- Phase 2 tuning design:
  - one-knob-at-a-time sweep
  - tune `replicator_update_every` first
  - tune `replicator_lr` second
  - tune `replicator_modulation_strength` third
- Phase 3 confidence design:
  - run toy baseline and toy replicator for 3 seeds
  - inspect trajectories, scheduler plots, and checks
  - confirm that collapse is avoided consistently
- Phase 4 ablation design:
  - main ablation: fast vs slow replicator
    - `replicator_update_every=1`
    - `replicator_update_every=10`
  - optional signal ablation:
    - `qm9_proxy`
    - `loss_gap`
  - collapse-vs-stable comparison:
    - old stress configuration on `init=1`
    - repaired stable configuration on `init=1`
- Non-toy design:
  - QM9 used as the primary benchmark attempt
  - one-seed, short/smoke configuration for:
    - `nashmtl`
    - `replicator_nashmtl`
  - objective: verify that both benchmark paths launch and behave sensibly before scaling up

Discussion

- The main contribution currently supported by the evidence is not benchmark superiority.
- The strongest confirmed contribution is a more stable and interpretable adaptive extension of Nash-MTL.
- The toy evidence supports three main claims:
  - plain Nash-MTL is a stable non-adaptive reference
  - replicator-NashMTL changes task weighting in a real and controlled way
  - the original collapse mode on the hard toy case has been repaired
- The weight-evolution figure is especially important because it shows that:
  - the outer scheduler is active
  - Nash still provides the inner bargaining structure
  - the replicator modulates rather than dominates the final weighting
- The fast-vs-slow ablation helps justify the scheduler design:
  - the update frequency materially affects responsiveness
  - this makes the outer scheduler behavior more interpretable
- The 3-seed toy pass increases confidence that the tuned toy setup is not a one-off artifact
- The optional signal ablation was less informative than expected because the final representative setup produced effectively identical outcomes for `qm9_proxy` and `loss_gap`
- This suggests:
  - the signal design may matter more in harsher or broader regimes
  - under the final fixed representative configuration, the dominant story is scheduler stability and responsiveness rather than signal-type separation

Limitations

- The strongest figures are still toy figures.
- The current evidence does not prove real benchmark superiority on QM9 or NYUv2.
- The toy setup validates mechanism and stability better than end-task performance.
- The non-toy QM9 path was launched and appears to enter training, but it did not yield a clean finished comparison figure within the bounded evaluation window.
- The optional `qm9_proxy` vs `loss_gap` ablation did not produce a strong separation under the final fixed representative setup.
- Because of this, the most defensible current claim is:
  - stability improved
  - adaptive behavior is now controlled and interpretable
  - benchmark-level performance improvement remains unconfirmed

Conclusion

- A replicator-augmented extension of Nash-MTL was developed and validated on a toy multi-objective setup.
- The final design keeps Nash-MTL as the per-step bargaining mechanism while adding a slower outer scheduler driven by EMA-smoothed task signals.
- The repaired design avoids the earlier collapse mode on the hard toy case and remains stable across seeds in the tuned toy harness.
- The toy evidence supports the method as a mechanism/stability improvement over the earlier replicator design, with the clearest confirmed gains in stability and controlled adaptive behavior.
- Real-data benchmark performance remains an open question because a clean completed non-toy comparison has not yet been obtained.
